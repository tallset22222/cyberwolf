# Cyberwolf character standard, part 3: body-hiding map (v0.2 draft)

Machine-readable source: `hiding_regions.json`. Diagram: `hiding_map.png`.

The base body mesh is split into **33 regions**, each a separate sub-mesh with one bit in a 64-bit mask. Every
implant, garment and chassis declares a hide mask. The body draws only regions whose bit is not set by anything worn.
This is the same idea as the chunk masks we decoded in Cyberpunk's character assembly, with our own regions.

**Status: validated baseline.** All 33 regions separate cleanly on hm08. There are no gaps or intersections between
neighbouring body regions at rest or in any pose: neighbours share vertices, so hiding cannot open a body-to-body gap.
Hiding any single region leaves a clean, manifold open edge (test 3). The 33 region IDs and their bits are kept, and
their current boundaries are the v0.2 baseline.

## Boundary types (v0.2)

v0.1 required every boundary to be an edge loop. hm08 shows that this is true for the limbs and the torso rings, but
not for the torso and head subdivisions. v0.2 therefore has two boundary types:

| type | definition | where | data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **edge loop** | a closed edge loop of the base mesh, referenced by seam or loop code | all limb boundaries, `neck_mid`, `neck_base`, `waist`, ribcage ring, forearm 15 %, shin 15 % | `topology/hm08/seams.json` |
| **drawn** | an explicit, versioned, ordered vertex list | mid-axillary lines, shoulder cap, pelvis front/back and gluteal fold, hairline, jawline, ears | `topology/hm08/boundaries.json` |

For hm08, `topology/hm08/regions.json` assigns every body face to its region. It is the authoritative split; the
boundary files describe it for review. All three files record the mesh's SHA-256 (`8e761e66…`) and use the original
OBJ vertex and face indices.

**Drawn boundaries are tied to one topology.** A vertex list is valid only for the mesh whose checksum it records. If
the base mesh is replaced, retopologised or edited (OI-11):

1. A topology change is a **breaking change** for the hiding map's binding, and must be versioned.
2. Every drawn boundary and the region face assignment must be **migrated explicitly**: transfer by surface proximity
   from the old mesh, then review by hand. They are never re-derived silently.
3. The migration is validated by re-running test 3 (separation, clean open edges, both sides) on the new mesh.
4. Edge-loop boundaries are re-found on the new mesh and their fractions re-measured with tests 1 and 3.

**Current quality of the drawn boundaries.** They were generated by geometric rules and are staircase paths
(jaggedness 1.16–2.52, where 1.0 is smooth). They are the baseline, and hand refinement is pending.

**Ears need special treatment.** hm08 has no edge loop at the ear root, and automatic detection did not isolate the
auricle. The current ear boundary is a **placeholder** that also takes temple and mastoid skin, and it must be authored
by hand (OI-12).

**Ribcage line: open (OI-13).** The ring used now sits 48 mm above the waist seam, leaving a thin `abdomen`. Clean
horizontal rings exist at 1,057–1,250 mm on hm08. The alternative is a drawn V following the lower ribcage. The two
are to be compared for topology quality and for behaviour under torso bending and twisting. A clean, predictable
attachment boundary is not sacrificed for anatomical precision.

## Hide rules

- **Replacements:** hide all regions distal to their seam. Example, the military leg from `thigh_mid_l`, left side:

  ```
  thigh_lower_l | knee_l | shin_l | foot_l
  = bits 18, 19, 20, 21
  = mask 0x00000000003c0000
  ```

- **Garments:** declare masks the same way. Thigh-high boots hide `knee`, `shin` and `foot`.
- **Combining items:** the mask a character renders with is the OR of everything worn.
- **Conflicts:** two items that replace the same region are a slot conflict and can't be worn together. Overlays and
  garments can stack over a replacement if they declare the replacement slot as compatible.
- **Layer order** (for clipping tests): body → replacement implants → overlays → under-layer clothing → outerwear → rigid items.

## Open points carried from v0.1

1. `face` hidden only by full masks. Half-masks (mouth/jaw plates): split `face` into upper and lower?
2. Torso uses front/back regions split at the mid-axillary line. A chest plate that wraps the sides hides `chest` + `back_upper`.
3. Are hands one region with fingers, or should fingers be separate (for finger-only implants)?
